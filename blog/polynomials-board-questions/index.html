Chapter 2 of CBSE Class 10 Maths, Polynomials, is short, but it shows up in almost every board paper, usually as 1 and 2 mark questions plus a case study. Nearly every question rests on just two formulas. If you can write them without thinking, you can finish this chapter's questions in a few minutes and bank the marks.

The two formulas you must know first

For a quadratic polynomial ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 0) with zeroes α and β:

| What | Formula | |---|---| | Sum of zeroes | α + β = −b/a | | Product of zeroes | αβ = c/a | | Polynomial from its zeroes | k[x² − (sum)x + (product)] |

A zero of a polynomial is a value of x that makes it equal to 0. On a graph, the zeroes are the points where the curve meets the x-axis.

Type 1: α + β + αβ (1 mark)

If α and β are the zeroes of p(x) = x² − ax − b, then (α + β + αβ) equals: (a) a + b (b) −a − b (c) a − b (d) −a + b

  • Write the coefficients first: x² → 1, x → −a, constant → −b.
  • α + β = −(−a)/1 = a, and αβ = −b/1 = −b.
  • Answer: (c) a − b.

Trap: confusing the letter a in the question with the coefficient a in the formula. Try it: α, β are zeroes of x² − 5x + 6. Find α + β − αβ. 5 − 6 = −1.

Type 2: Pick the zeroes without solving (1 mark)

Zeroes of p(x) = x² − 3√2x + 4 are: (a) 2, √2 (b) 2√2, √2 (c) 4√2, −√2 (d) √2, 2

  • The sum must be 3√2 and the product must be 4.
  • (a): 2 + √2 is not 3√2. ✗
  • (b): 2√2 + √2 = 3√2 ✓ and 2√2 × √2 = 4 ✓
  • Answer: (b) 2√2, √2.

Trap: checking only the product or only the sum. Always test both. Try it: find the zeroes of x² − 2√3x + 3. √3, √3.

Type 3: Find p from a condition (1 mark)

If α and β are the zeroes of f(x) = px² − 2x + 3p and α + β = αβ, then p is: (a) −2/3 (b) 2/3 (c) 1/3 (d) −1/3

  • a = p, b = −2, c = 3p.
  • α + β = −(−2)/p = 2/p, and αβ = 3p/p = 3.
  • 2/p = 3, so 3p = 2 and p = 2/3.
  • Answer: (b) 2/3.

Trap: writing the sum as −2/p. Minus of minus 2 is plus 2. Try it: the product of the zeroes of kx² + 4x + 5 is 3. Find k. 5/k = 3, so k = 5/3.

Type 4: Assertion and Reason, reciprocal zeroes (1 mark)

Assertion (A): If the zeroes of (2k − 1)x² + 4x − 3 are reciprocals of each other, then k = −1.
Reason (R): If a = c, then the zeroes of ax² + bx + c, a ≠ 0, are reciprocals of each other.

  • Reciprocal zeroes are α and 1/α, so their product is 1. Product = c/a = 1 means a = c. R is true.
  • Here 2k − 1 = −3, so 2k = −2 and k = −1. A is true.
  • A was solved using exactly the condition in R.
  • Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

Trap: finding k correctly and then not checking whether R actually explains A. Try it: for what k are the zeroes of kx² + 3x + 5 reciprocals? k = 5.

Type 5: A big α-β expression (2 marks)

If α and β are the zeroes of p(y) = y² − 5y + 3, find α⁴β³ + α³β⁴.

  • α + β = 5 and αβ = 3.
  • Take out the common factor: α⁴β³ + α³β⁴ = α³β³(α + β) = (αβ)³(α + β).
  • = 3³ × 5 = 27 × 5 = 135.

Trap: trying to find α and β separately. They are irrational here, and it wastes time. Try it: same polynomial, find α²β + αβ². αβ(α + β) = 3 × 5 = 15.

Type 6: α³β + β³α in letters (2 marks)

If α and β are the zeroes of px² + qx + r, find α³β + β³α.

  • α + β = −q/p and αβ = r/p.
  • α³β + β³α = αβ(α² + β²), and α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = (q² − 2pr)/p².
  • So α³β + β³α = (r/p) × (q² − 2pr)/p² = r(q² − 2pr)/p³.

Trap: writing α² + β² = (α + β)². Do not forget the −2αβ. Try it: α, β are zeroes of x² − 7x + 10. Find α² + β². 49 − 20 = 29.

Type 7: Find the zeroes, then build a new polynomial (3 marks)

Find the zeroes of p(x) = 3x² − 4x − 4. Hence write a polynomial whose zeroes are each 2 more than the zeroes of p(x).

  • Split the middle term: 3x² − 6x + 2x − 4 = 3x(x − 2) + 2(x − 2) = (3x + 2)(x − 2).
  • Zeroes: 2 and −2/3.
  • New zeroes: 2 + 2 = 4 and −2/3 + 2 = 4/3.
  • Sum = 16/3, product = 16/3, so the polynomial is k[x² − (16/3)x + 16/3]. Take k = 3: 3x² − 16x + 16.

Check: 3(4)² − 16(4) + 16 = 48 − 64 + 16 = 0. ✓

Trap: adding 2 to the coefficients instead of to the zeroes. Try it: find a quadratic polynomial whose zeroes are 3 and −5. x² + 2x − 15.

Type 8: Case study, the arch (4 marks)

A theatre backdrop shows arches whose curve is p(x) = −x² + 2x + 8, where x is the length in feet along the stage. (i) Find the height of the arch. (ii) Find the zeroes of p(x) and the span of the arch. (iii) Where does the curve meet the y-axis?

  • Zeroes: −x² + 2x + 8 = 0 means x² − 2x − 8 = 0, so (x − 4)(x + 2) = 0 and the zeroes are 4 and −2.
  • Span = 4 − (−2) = 6 ft.
  • The top of the arch is midway between the zeroes, at x = 1. Height = p(1) = −1 + 2 + 8 = 9 ft.
  • On the y-axis x = 0, so p(0) = 8: the point (0, 8).

Trap: taking the y-intercept (8) as the height. The top is at x = 1, not x = 0. Try it: for the same arch, find p(3). −9 + 6 + 8 = 5 ft.

One-minute revision

  • Sum of zeroes = −b/a; product of zeroes = c/a. Write a, b, c down before you start.
  • In an MCQ, test each option with both the sum and the product.
  • Reciprocal zeroes: product = 1, so a = c.
  • α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ. Factor big α-β expressions before substituting.
  • From zeroes: k[x² − (sum)x + (product)].
  • Graph: zeroes are where the curve meets the x-axis; a downward arch is highest midway between them.

FAQ

How many marks does Polynomials carry in the board exam? Polynomials is part of the Algebra unit, which CBSE gives the largest share of the Class 10 Maths paper. From this chapter expect mostly 1 and 2 mark questions, often with a case study or an assertion-reason item like the ones above.

Do I need to learn the cubic polynomial relations? The current NCERT book focuses on quadratic polynomials for the relationship between zeroes and coefficients, so master the two formulas above first. Every question in this post uses only those.
