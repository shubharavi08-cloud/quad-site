Metals and Non-metals Class 10: 7 Must-Do Board Questions, Solved (CBSE Science Chapter 3, Reactivity Series)

By Team QUAD19 Nov 20267 min read

▶ Metals and Non-metals Class 10 Important Questions: 7 must-do questions + MCQ tricks · open on YouTube

Chapter 3 of CBSE Class 10 Science, Metals and Non-metals, looks like a chapter of facts to memorise. In the board paper it is really a chapter about one list: the reactivity series. Once you know where a metal sits in that list, most questions answer themselves: does it react with cold water, does it give hydrogen with an acid, can it push another metal out of a salt solution, how is it extracted?

Below are seven question types from recent CBSE papers. The video above solves all seven. Read each question, pause and try it yourself, then check the model answer and the trap.

The one list you must know first

K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au

A popular memory line: "Please Send Charlie's Monkeys And Zebras In Large Herds Cause Mischief Sometimes Gently", or make your own. What the position tells you:

| Position | Cold water | Dilute HCl | How it is extracted | |---|---|---|---| | K, Na, Ca | Reacts (K and Na violently) | Gives H₂ | Electrolysis of the molten chloride | | Mg, Al | Mg: hot water only. Al: steam | Gives H₂ | Electrolysis | | Zn, Fe, Pb | Zn, Fe: steam only. Pb: none | Gives H₂ (Pb only slowly) | Reduce the oxide with carbon | | Cu, Hg, Ag, Au | No reaction | No H₂ (below hydrogen) | Cu, Hg: heat alone. Au: found free |

Type 1: Which metal does not react with cold water? (1 mark)

Which one of the following metals does not react with cold water? (a) Sodium (b) Potassium (c) Calcium (d) Magnesium

  • Potassium and sodium react violently with cold water: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ (plus a lot of heat).
  • Calcium reacts too, less violently: Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂.
  • Magnesium does not react with cold water. It reacts only with hot water.
  • Answer: (d) Magnesium.

Trap: picking calcium. Calcium does react with cold water, just more gently than sodium. Try it: which metal reacts only with steam: Na, Ca or Fe? Iron.

Type 2: Why does magnesium float in hot water? (1 mark)

When Mg metal is treated with hot water, it starts floating on the surface. The reason is: (a) Mg is lighter than water (b) Mg reacts with water to produce H₂ gas, which helps in floating (c) … N₂ gas … (d) … CO₂ gas …

  • Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂.
  • Bubbles of hydrogen stick to the surface of the metal and lift it up.
  • Answer: (b).

Trap: picking (a). Magnesium is about 1.7 times as dense as water. It floats only because of the hydrogen bubbles. Try it: which other metal floats for the same reason? Calcium.

Type 3: The alloy that contains a non-metal (1 mark)

Which of these alloys contains a non-metal as one of its constituents? (a) Brass (b) Bronze (c) Amalgam (d) Steel

  • Brass = copper + zinc. Bronze = copper + tin. Both metals only.
  • Amalgam = mercury + another metal. Mercury is a liquid metal.
  • Steel = iron + a small amount of carbon, and carbon is a non-metal.
  • Answer: (d) Steel.

Trap: picking amalgam because mercury "feels different". It is still a metal. Remember: solder = lead + tin; stainless steel = iron + nickel + chromium (+ a little carbon).

Type 4: Which pair gives hydrogen with dilute HCl? (1 mark)

On reaction with dilute HCl, which pair of metals will evolve hydrogen gas? (a) copper and zinc (b) copper and iron (c) silver and magnesium (d) magnesium and aluminium

  • Only metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series displace it from an acid.
  • Copper and silver sit below hydrogen, so any pair that contains one of them is wrong.
  • Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ and 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂.
  • Answer: (d) magnesium and aluminium.

Trap: seeing zinc or magnesium in a pair and ticking it. One "no" metal makes the whole pair wrong. Try it: why do most metals not give hydrogen with nitric acid? HNO₃ is a strong oxidising agent: it oxidises the H₂ produced to water.

Type 5: Iron filings in four salt solutions (2 marks)

Iron filings are added to four test tubes A, B, C and D containing ZnSO₄, CuSO₄, FeSO₄ and Al₂(SO₄)₃ solutions. (i) In which tube is the reaction most vigorous, and why? (ii) Write the balanced equation.

  • A metal can displace only a less reactive metal from its salt solution.
  • Zinc and aluminium are more reactive than iron (A and D: no reaction). Tube C already contains iron: nothing to displace.
  • Iron is more reactive than copper, so in tube B it pushes copper out.
  • Answer: (i) Test tube B, because iron is more reactive than copper. (ii) Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. The blue solution turns pale green and a reddish-brown coat of copper forms on the iron.

Trap: choosing A or D because zinc and aluminium are "strong" metals. Being stronger is exactly why iron cannot displace them. Try it: will copper displace silver from AgNO₃ solution? Yes: Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag.

Type 6: Give reasons: carbon, ionic compounds, solder (3 marks)

(a) Why can the oxides of highly reactive metals not be reduced by carbon? (b) Why do ionic compounds have high melting and boiling points? (c) Why is solder used for joining electrical wires?

  • (a) Metals such as Na, Ca, Mg and Al have more affinity for oxygen than carbon has, so carbon cannot take the oxygen away. They are obtained by electrolysis of their molten chlorides.
  • (b) The ions are held together by strong electrostatic forces of attraction; a large amount of energy is needed to break them.
  • (c) Solder (lead + tin) has a low melting point, so it melts easily and joins the wires without damaging them.

Trap: in (c), writing "solder is a good conductor". The marking point is the low melting point. Try it: why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis and not with carbon? It has more affinity for oxygen than carbon.

Type 7: Forming MgO by transfer of electrons (3 marks)

(i) Name a metal soft enough to cut with a knife, and a non-metal that conducts electricity. (ii) Using electronic configurations, show how Mg combines with O to form magnesium oxide. [Mg = 12, O = 8]

  • (i) Soft metal: sodium (or potassium). Non-metal that conducts: graphite (a form of carbon).
  • (ii) Mg (2, 8, 2) loses 2 electrons → Mg²⁺ (2, 8).
  • O (2, 6) gains 2 electrons → O²⁻ (2, 8).
  • Both now have a complete octet. The opposite charges attract: Mg²⁺O²⁻, an ionic (electrovalent) bond.

Trap: drawing shared electron pairs, as for a covalent bond. In MgO the electrons are transferred: draw the arrow from Mg to O and write the charges. Try it: show the electron transfer in NaCl (Na = 11, Cl = 17). Na (2, 8, 1) loses 1e⁻ → Na⁺; Cl (2, 8, 7) gains it → Cl⁻.

One-minute revision

  • Reactivity series: K Na Ca Mg Al Zn Fe Pb [H] Cu Hg Ag Au.
  • Cold water: K, Na, Ca. Hot water: Mg. Steam: Al, Zn, Fe. None: Pb, Cu, Ag, Au.
  • Metals above H give H₂ with dilute HCl; Cu, Hg, Ag, Au do not. Nitric acid usually gives no H₂.
  • A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive one from its salt solution (Fe + CuSO₄).
  • Extraction: top of the series by electrolysis, middle by carbon reduction, bottom by heat alone.
  • Alloys: steel (Fe + C), brass (Cu + Zn), bronze (Cu + Sn), solder (Pb + Sn, low melting point).

FAQ

Do I need to learn the whole reactivity series in order? Yes. It is the fastest way to answer MCQs on water, acids, displacement and extraction, and it is only thirteen symbols.
