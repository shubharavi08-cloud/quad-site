Human Eye and the Colourful World Class 10: 7 Must-Do Board Questions, Solved (CBSE Science Chapter 10, Myopia and Hypermetropia)

By Team QUAD20 Nov 20268 min read

▶ Human Eye and Colourful World Class 10 Important Questions: 7 must-do questions + MCQ tricks · open on YouTube

Chapter 10 of CBSE Class 10 Science, The Human Eye and the Colourful World, is one of the shortest chapters in the book and one of the most reliable for marks. The board paper asks almost the same things every year: which defect, which lens, why, one prism diagram and one question on scattering. If you can answer "where does the image form?" for each defect, you can answer most of the chapter.

Below are seven question types from recent CBSE papers. The video above solves all seven. Read each question, pause and try it yourself, then check the model answer and the trap.

The one table you must know first

| Defect | What goes wrong | Where the image forms | Corrective lens | |---|---|---|---| | Myopia (near-sightedness) | Eyeball too long, or eye lens too curved | In front of the retina | Concave (diverging), negative power | | Hypermetropia (far-sightedness) | Eyeball too short, or focal length of the lens too long | Behind the retina | Convex (converging), positive power | | Presbyopia (old age) | Ciliary muscles weaken, the lens gets less flexible | Near point moves away | Convex for reading; often bifocal |

A normal eye sees clearly from 25 cm (the near point) to infinity (the far point). The ability of the eye lens to change its focal length for this is the power of accommodation.

Type 1: Weak ciliary muscles: which defect? (1 mark)

The vision defect which arises due to gradual weakening of the ciliary muscles and diminishing flexibility of the eye lens is: (a) Myopia (b) Hypermetropia (c) Presbyopia (d) Myopia and hypermetropia both

  • The ciliary muscles change the shape of the eye lens (accommodation).
  • With age these muscles weaken and the lens becomes stiff, so the power of accommodation falls and the near point recedes.
  • That age-related defect is presbyopia.
  • Answer: (c) Presbyopia.

Trap: picking (d). Some older people have both myopia and hypermetropia and need bifocal lenses, but the defect caused by weak ciliary muscles is presbyopia. Try it: what kind of lens is the lower part of a bifocal? Convex, for reading near objects.

Type 2: A shortened eyeball needs which lens? (1 mark)

To restore clear vision in persons whose size of the eyeball has reduced, he/she is suggested to use a suitable: (a) converging lens (b) diverging lens (c) bifocal lens (d) cylindrical lens

  • A shorter eyeball means the image of a near object forms behind the retina. That is hypermetropia.
  • The eye needs extra converging power, so a convex lens brings the image forward onto the retina.
  • Answer: (a) Converging lens.

Trap: picking "diverging". A diverging lens spreads the rays further and pushes the image even further behind the retina. Try it: is the power of this lens positive or negative? Positive (convex lens).

Type 3: Accommodation and its range (2 marks)

(i) How does the change in curvature of the eye lens help us see nearby objects clearly? (ii) State the range of the power of accommodation of a normal human eye.

  • (i) For a near object the ciliary muscles contract, the lens becomes thicker (more curved), its focal length decreases, and the image still forms on the retina. For distant objects the muscles relax and the lens becomes thin.
  • (ii) A normal eye sees clearly from 25 cm (near point) to infinity (far point).

Trap: writing that the lens moves closer to the retina. In the eye the lens changes shape, not position. (In a camera the lens moves; in your eye it does not.) Try it: what is the near point of a normal adult eye? About 25 cm.

Type 4: Why a concave lens fixes myopia (2 marks)

Why is a concave lens used as a corrective lens for a myopic eye?

  • In myopia, distant objects look blurred because their image forms in front of the retina.
  • A concave (diverging) lens of suitable power spreads the rays slightly, so they meet a little farther back, exactly on the retina.

Trap: writing only "it diverges light". The marks are for saying where the image moves: from in front of the retina onto it. Try it: a myopic person's far point is 2 m. What is the power of the corrective lens? f = −2 m, so P = 1/f = −0.5 D.

Type 5: The elongated eyeball (3 marks)

A person is suffering from a defect of vision due to the elongation of his/her eyeball. (a) Write the name of the defect and the other possible cause of this defect. (b) Draw the ray diagram for the correction of this defect.

  • (a) Elongated eyeball means myopia (near-sightedness). The other cause is excessive curvature of the eye lens.
  • (b) In the diagram, show parallel rays from a distant object, a concave lens in front of the eye, and the rays now meeting on the retina. Label the retina and the lens, and draw arrows on every ray.

Trap: drawing a convex lens. Elongated eyeball = myopia = concave. Say it to yourself as one line. Try it: what happens to the far point of a myopic eye? It comes closer than infinity.

Type 6: Through a prism: the angle of deviation (3 marks)

(a) Draw the ray diagram for refraction of light through a glass prism and mark the angle of refraction and the angle of deviation. (b) When the path of a light ray refracted through a glass prism is reversed, how will the angle of deviation change? Explain.

  • (a) The incident ray bends towards the normal as it enters the glass (angle of refraction r inside). It bends away from the normal as it leaves. The angle of deviation D is the angle between the incident ray extended forward and the emergent ray.
  • (b) The deviation stays the same. Light paths are reversible: a ray sent back along the emergent path retraces the same path through the prism.

Trap: forgetting to extend the incident ray. D is measured between the extended incident ray and the emergent ray, so draw that dotted line. Try it: which colour is deviated the most by a prism? Violet (red the least).

Type 7: Scattering vs reflection (3 marks)

What is scattering of light? How is scattering of light different from reflection of light? Explain.

  • Scattering: light striking very small particles (such as air molecules or fine dust) is spread out in all directions. How much is scattered depends on the size of the particles and the wavelength of the light: very fine particles scatter mainly blue light.
  • Reflection: light bounces off a surface in one definite direction, following the laws of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection).
  • Examples worth one line each: the sky looks blue because air molecules scatter blue light more than red; the sun looks reddish at sunrise and sunset because its light crosses a longer path of air and most of the blue is scattered away.

Trap: calling scattering "reflection in many directions". Scattering is by tiny particles and depends on wavelength; reflection is by a surface. Try it: why does the sky look dark to an astronaut in space? There is no atmosphere to scatter sunlight.

One-minute revision

  • Near point 25 cm, far point infinity; the eye lens changes focal length using the ciliary muscles.
  • Myopia: image in front of the retina; long eyeball or too-curved lens; concave lens.
  • Hypermetropia: image behind the retina; short eyeball or long focal length; convex lens.
  • Presbyopia: old age, weak ciliary muscles; convex or bifocal lens.
  • Power P = 1/f (f in metres): concave lens, negative D; convex lens, positive D.
  • Prism: D between the extended incident ray and the emergent ray; violet deviates most.
  • Scattering: blue sky, reddish sunrise and sunset, a dark sky in space.

FAQ

Do I need to draw the defect and the correction separately? If the question says "draw the ray diagram for the correction", one clear diagram with the corrective lens is enough. If it says "illustrate the defect and its correction", draw both: first the image in the wrong place, then with the lens.

Is the "colourful world" part asked in the board exam? Yes. Dispersion through a prism, the blue sky and the reddish sun at sunrise are regular 1 to 3 mark questions, as in Types 6 and 7.
