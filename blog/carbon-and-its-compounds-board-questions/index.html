Carbon and its Compounds: 7 Board Question Types, Solved (CBSE Class 10 Science Chapter 4)

By Team QUAD12 Nov 20265 min read

▶ Carbon and its Compounds Class 10: 7 must-do board questions + MCQ tricks · open on YouTube

Chapter 4 of CBSE Class 10 Science, Carbon and its Compounds, looks long, but the board keeps returning to the same seven ideas. Students lose marks by mixing up isomers and homologues, by forgetting reaction conditions (443 K, concentrated H₂SO₄, acid catalyst) and by writing "carbon forms ions", which it does not.

Read each type, pause and try it yourself, then check the model answer. Every question type below came in the CBSE 2026 board papers.

Type 1: Spot the odd pair in a homologous series

A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same functional group and the same general formula, where each member differs from the next by CH₂ (14 u).

Which pair is NOT a pair of successive homologues: CH₄ and C₂H₆, C₂H₄ and C₃H₆, or C₄H₈ and C₅H₁₂?

  • CH₄ and C₂H₆ are both alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂) and differ by CH₂: homologues.
  • C₂H₄ and C₃H₆ are both alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ) and differ by CH₂: homologues.
  • C₄H₈ is an alkene but C₅H₁₂ is an alkane, so they belong to different families: this is the odd pair.

Exam shortcut: check the general formula first. If the two formulas fit different families, you are done in seconds.

Type 2: What happens inside a soap micelle

Why does soap form micelles, and where does the oily dirt go?

  • A soap molecule has a long hydrocarbon tail (hydrophobic, dissolves in oil) and an ionic head (hydrophilic, dissolves in water).
  • In water, the tails point inwards into the oil drop and the heads face outwards into the water. This cluster is a micelle.
  • The micelle traps the oily dirt in its centre, and rinsing washes it away.

Common mistake: drawing the heads inside. The water-loving part always faces the water.

Try it: why does soap not lather well in hard water? Calcium and magnesium ions in hard water react with soap and form an insoluble scum.

Type 3: Isomers of butane

Isomers have the same molecular formula but different structures.

  • Straight-chain butane: CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CH₃
  • Branched isomer (2-methylpropane): CH₃–CH(CH₃)–CH₃
  • Both are C₄H₁₀, so they are isomers, not homologues. Homologues always differ by CH₂.

Try it: pentane (C₅H₁₂) has three structural isomers.

Type 4: Which solutions conduct electricity?

Bulbs are connected through solutions of fructose, ethanol, dilute HCl and NaOH. Which bulbs glow?

  • HCl and NaOH form ions in water, so they conduct and the bulb glows.
  • Fructose and ethanol are covalent compounds; they do not form ions, so they do not conduct.

Marking tip: the one-line reason ("ionic compounds give ions in solution; covalent compounds do not") carries the mark. Distilled water also conducts very poorly because it has almost no ions.

Type 5: Assertion-reason on glacial acetic acid

Assertion: pure ethanoic acid is called glacial acetic acid. Reason: its melting point is 290 K, so it often freezes in cold climates.

  • Pure ethanoic acid melts at 290 K (about 17 °C). In winter it freezes into ice-like crystals, hence "glacial".
  • Both statements are true and the reason explains the assertion.

Try it: vinegar is a 5–8 % solution of ethanoic acid in water.

Type 6: Identify the compound P (5 marks)

A classic five-marker gives clues and asks you to name the compound and write equations. When the clues are "a liquid used in medicines and tincture, reacts with sodium, gives an alkene with hot concentrated sulphuric acid", P is ethanol, C₂H₅OH.

  • With sodium: 2Na + 2CH₃CH₂OH → 2CH₃CH₂ONa + H₂. The hydrogen burns with a pop sound.
  • With excess concentrated H₂SO₄ at 443 K: CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O. Here the acid is a dehydrating agent.
  • With ethanoic acid and an acid catalyst (warm): CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O. The ester has a sweet smell (esterification).

Try it: what is saponification? Heating an ester with sodium hydroxide gives back the alcohol and the sodium salt of the acid (sodium ethanoate). This is how soap is made.

Type 7: Why carbon shares electrons

Why does carbon form covalent bonds instead of C⁴⁺ or C⁴⁻ ions?

  • To form C⁴⁻, a nucleus with 6 protons would have to hold 10 electrons, which is hard.
  • To form C⁴⁺, it would have to lose 4 electrons, which needs a very large amount of energy.
  • So carbon shares its four valence electrons, forming covalent bonds.

The same five-marker usually adds: homologues differ by CH₂, and alkaline KMnO₄ or acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid. Acidified potassium dichromate turns from orange to green.

Five-minute revision checklist

  • Homologues: same functional group, differ by CH₂. Isomers: same formula, different structure.
  • Micelle: tails in, heads out.
  • Ionic solutions conduct; sugar and alcohol solutions do not.
  • 443 K + conc. H₂SO₄ = ethene. Acid + alcohol = ester. Ester + NaOH = soap reaction.
  • Carbon shares electrons: no C⁴⁺, no C⁴⁻.

FAQ

Is Carbon and its Compounds important for the 2026-27 boards? Yes. It appeared in every 2026 set, usually as MCQs plus one long answer on ethanol or ethanoic acid.

How do I remember the conditions? Link each condition to its product: 443 K with concentrated acid makes ethene; an acid catalyst with ethanoic acid makes an ester.
