Real Numbers Class 10: 8 Must-Do Board Questions, Solved (CBSE Maths Chapter 1, HCF, LCM and Irrational Proofs)

By Team QUAD23 Nov 20267 min read

▶ Class 10 Maths Real Numbers Important Questions: 8 Must-Do Board Questions · open on YouTube

Chapter 1 of CBSE Class 10 Maths, Real Numbers, is the first chapter you study and one of the easiest places to score in the board paper. It has only two big ideas: prime factorisation (for HCF and LCM) and proving a number is irrational. Almost every question is one of the eight types below.

The rules you must know first

| What | Rule | |---|---| | Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic | Every composite number is a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from order) | | HCF | Only the common primes, each with its lowest power | | LCM | Every prime that appears, each with its highest power | | Two numbers a and b | HCF × LCM = a × b | | Co-prime numbers | HCF = 1, so LCM = their product |

And for word problems: "greatest number that divides" or "longest that measures exactly" means HCF; "smallest number divisible by" or "together again" means LCM.

Type 1: LCM of three numbers (1 mark)

The LCM of three numbers 28, 44 and 132 is: (a) 258 (b) 231 (c) 462 (d) 924

  • 28 = 2² × 7, 44 = 2² × 11, 132 = 2² × 3 × 11.
  • LCM = every prime, highest power = 2² × 3 × 7 × 11.
  • 4 × 3 = 12, 12 × 7 = 84, 84 × 11 = 924.
  • Answer: (d) 924.

Trap: multiplying all three numbers. That gives a common multiple, not the least one. Try it: find the LCM of 12, 15 and 21. 2² × 3 × 5 × 7 = 420.

Type 2: Co-prime numbers (1 mark)

If the product of two co-prime numbers is 553, then their HCF is: (a) 1 (b) 553 (c) 7 (d) 79

  • Co-prime means the only common factor is 1.
  • So the HCF is 1, whatever the product is. (553 = 7 × 79 is only there to distract you.)
  • Answer: (a) 1.

Trap: factorising 553 and picking 7 or 79. Try it: two co-prime numbers have product 117. Find their LCM. For co-primes, LCM = product = 117.

Type 3: Greatest number with remainders (1 mark)

The greatest number which divides both 134 and 188, leaving remainders 4 and 6 respectively, is: (a) 13 (b) 26 (c) 39 (d) 65

  • "Greatest number that divides" means HCF.
  • Take away the remainders first: 134 − 4 = 130 and 188 − 6 = 182.
  • 130 = 2 × 5 × 13 and 182 = 2 × 7 × 13, so HCF = 2 × 13 = 26.
  • Answer: (b) 26.

Trap: forgetting to subtract the remainders. 13 also divides both, but it is not the greatest. Try it: the greatest number dividing 281 and 1249, leaving remainders 5 and 7. HCF of 276 and 1242 = 2 × 3 × 23 = 138.

Type 4: Assertion and Reason, HCF × LCM (1 mark)

Assertion (A): If the product of two numbers is 5780 and their HCF is 17, then their LCM is 340.
Reason (R): HCF is always a factor of LCM.

  • A: HCF × LCM = product, so LCM = 5780 ÷ 17 = 340. A is true.
  • R: the HCF divides both numbers, and the LCM is a multiple of both, so the HCF divides the LCM. R is true.
  • But A was solved with the product rule, not with R.
  • Answer: (b) Both A and R are true, but R is not the correct explanation of A.

Trap: choosing (a) just because both statements are true. Always ask: does R explain A? Try it: can two numbers have HCF 24 and LCM 540? No: 540 ÷ 24 = 22.5 is not a whole number, but the HCF must divide the LCM.

Type 5: The longest ruler (2 marks)

A hall is 9.75 m long, 6.75 m wide and 5.25 m high. What is the length of the longest unmarked ruler that can exactly measure all three dimensions?

  • "Longest that measures exactly" means HCF.
  • Remove the decimals: 975 cm, 675 cm, 525 cm.
  • 975 = 3 × 5² × 13, 675 = 3³ × 5², 525 = 3 × 5² × 7.
  • Common primes, lowest powers: 3 × 5² = 75.
  • Answer: the longest ruler is 75 cm.

Trap: writing just "75". Always give the unit: 75 cm (or 0.75 m). Try it: a school has 48 French, 80 Hindi and 144 English teachers. Each room must hold teachers of one subject only, the same number in every room. What is the least number of rooms? HCF = 16 per room, so 3 + 5 + 9 = 17 rooms.

Type 6: Bells tolling together (2 marks)

Three bells toll at intervals of 5, 6 and 8 minutes. They toll together when the park opens at 10:00 a.m. How many more times do they toll together until the park closes at 7:00 p.m.?

  • "Together again" means LCM.
  • 5 = 5, 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³, so LCM = 2³ × 3 × 5 = 120 minutes = 2 hours.
  • From 10 a.m. they toll together at 12 noon, 2 p.m., 4 p.m. and 6 p.m. (8 p.m. is after closing.)
  • Answer: 4 more times.

Trap: writing 9 ÷ 2 = 4.5, or counting the 10 a.m. toll and answering 5. The question says more times. Try it: traffic lights change every 48, 72 and 108 seconds and change together at 7 a.m. When do they next change together? LCM = 2⁴ × 3³ = 432 s = 7 min 12 s, so at 7:07:12 a.m.

Type 7: Prove that √5 is irrational (3 to 5 marks)

Prove that √5 is an irrational number.

  1. Assume √5 is rational. Then √5 = p/q, where p and q are co-prime integers and q ≠ 0.
  2. Squaring, 5 = p²/q², so p² = 5q².
  3. So 5 divides p². Since 5 is prime, 5 divides p.
  4. Let p = 5m. Then 25m² = 5q², so q² = 5m².
  5. So 5 divides q², and therefore 5 divides q.
  6. Now 5 divides both p and q, but p and q are co-prime. This is a contradiction.
  7. So our assumption was wrong: √5 is irrational. Hence proved.

Trap: skipping the reason "5 is prime, so 5 divides p". Examiners give marks for that line. Try it: prove that √3 is irrational. Same steps, with 3 in place of 5.

Type 8: Prove that 2 + 3√3 is irrational (3 marks)

Given that √3 is irrational, prove that 2 + 3√3 is irrational.

  1. Assume 2 + 3√3 = r, where r is rational.
  2. Then 3√3 = r − 2, so √3 = (r − 2)/3.
  3. The right side is rational, because r − 2 and 3 are rational. So √3 would be rational.
  4. But √3 is irrational (given). This is a contradiction.
  5. So 2 + 3√3 is irrational. Hence proved.

Trap: writing only "rational + irrational is irrational". That is a fact, not a proof. Show the steps. Try it: given √2 is irrational, prove 5 + 3√2 is irrational. Same steps: √2 = (r − 5)/3.

One-minute revision

  • HCF: common primes, lowest powers. LCM: all primes, highest powers.
  • HCF × LCM = product works for two numbers only.
  • Co-prime: HCF = 1 and LCM = product.
  • Remainders: subtract them first, then find the HCF.
  • Decimals in a word problem: change to whole units (cm) first, and keep the unit in the answer.
  • Irrational proofs: assume → square → divides → contradiction, and say why the prime divides p.

FAQ

How many marks does Real Numbers carry in the board exam? Real Numbers is a unit of its own in the CBSE Class 10 Maths syllabus, with about 6 marks. Expect one or two MCQs or an assertion-reason item, a 2-mark HCF/LCM word problem, and often an irrational-number proof.

Is Euclid's division algorithm still asked? The current NCERT book (rationalised syllabus) teaches HCF and LCM through prime factorisation (the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic). Euclid's division lemma was removed, so practise the method used in this post.
