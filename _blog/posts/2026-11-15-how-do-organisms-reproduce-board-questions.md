## Type 1: DNA copying and why variation matters

> Why is DNA copying an essential part of reproduction? What is the importance of variation?

- The **DNA in the nucleus** carries the information for making proteins, and so for the body design of the organism.
- Reproduction makes a **copy of the DNA** and passes it on, so the new cell gets the same blueprint.
- The copying is **not perfectly accurate**. Small changes creep in, and these are the source of **variation**.
- **Importance of variation:** if the environment changes (for example, the water gets hotter), most individuals of a population may die, but a few **variants** that can survive may live on. Variation helps a **species survive over time**.

## Type 2: Fission and fragmentation

> Distinguish between binary fission and multiple fission, with one example of each.

| Method | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Binary fission | One cell splits into **two** equal daughter cells | *Amoeba* (any plane), *Leishmania* (definite plane) |
| Multiple fission | One cell divides into **many** daughter cells at the same time | *Plasmodium* (malaria parasite) |
| Fragmentation | A mature filament **breaks into pieces**, and each piece grows into a new individual | *Spirogyra* |

**Common mistake:** writing "Amoeba" for multiple fission. Amoeba = binary, Plasmodium = multiple.

## Type 3: Regeneration and budding

> What is regeneration? Why can't higher organisms reproduce by regeneration?

- **Regeneration:** a fully formed organism is cut into pieces, and each piece **grows into a complete organism**. Examples: ***Hydra*** and ***Planaria***. Specialised cells divide and change into the different cell types needed.
- **Budding:** a small **outgrowth (bud)** forms because of repeated cell division at one site, grows into a tiny individual and **detaches** when mature. Examples: ***Hydra*** and **yeast**.
- Higher organisms cannot reproduce by regeneration because their bodies are **complex**, with organs and organ systems made of highly specialised cells. A piece of the body cannot rebuild all of them.

**Try it:** Hydra appears in two methods. Which ones? **Regeneration** and **budding**.

## Type 4: Spore formation and vegetative propagation

> How does *Rhizopus* reproduce? Give two advantages of vegetative propagation.

- ***Rhizopus*** (bread mould) forms **sporangia**, round blobs on thin threads. Each sporangium holds many **spores**. Spores have **thick walls** that protect them until they reach a moist surface, where they grow into new plants.
- **Vegetative propagation:** new plants grow from **roots, stems or leaves** of the parent. *Bryophyllum* grows new plants from **buds in the notches of its leaves**; sugarcane, roses and grapes are grown from **stem cuttings or layering**.
- **Advantages:** plants grow and **flower earlier** than those grown from seed; plants that have **lost the ability to make seeds** (banana, orange, rose, jasmine) can still be grown; all the new plants are **genetically like the parent**, so a good variety stays the same.

## Type 5: The flower, pollination and fertilisation

> Draw a longitudinal section of a flower. Distinguish between pollination and fertilisation.

- **Male part: stamen** (anther + filament); the anther makes **pollen grains**.
- **Female part: pistil (carpel)** = **stigma**, **style** and **ovary**; the ovary holds **ovules**, each with an **egg cell**.
- **Unisexual** flowers have either stamens or pistil (papaya, watermelon). **Bisexual** flowers have both (*Hibiscus*, mustard).

| Pollination | Fertilisation |
|---|---|
| Transfer of **pollen from anther to stigma** | **Fusion of the male germ cell with the egg** inside the ovule |
| Happens **outside** the ovule | Happens **inside** the ovary |
| Carried by wind, water or animals | A **pollen tube** grows down the style to reach the ovule |

**After fertilisation:** the **zygote** grows into an **embryo**, the **ovule becomes the seed**, and the **ovary becomes the fruit**. Petals, sepals, stamens, style and stigma may shrivel and fall off.

## Type 6: Human reproductive system

> Describe the functions of the testes and the placenta. What happens when the egg is not fertilised?

- **Testes** make **sperm** and the hormone **testosterone**. They sit in the **scrotum**, outside the abdomen, because sperm formation needs a **lower temperature** than body temperature.
- Sperm travel through the **vas deferens**; the **seminal vesicles** and **prostate gland** add fluid that makes transport easier and gives nutrition.
- **Ovaries** release **one egg every month** and make hormones. Fertilisation takes place in the **oviduct (fallopian tube)**.
- The fertilised egg (zygote) implants in the thick, blood-rich lining of the **uterus**. The **placenta**, a disc-shaped tissue, carries **glucose and oxygen from the mother** to the embryo and **removes its wastes**.
- If the egg is **not fertilised**, the thick uterine lining is not needed. It breaks down and comes out as blood and mucus: **menstruation**, roughly every month.

## Type 7: Reproductive health

> List the methods of contraception. Why is prenatal sex determination prohibited?

| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| Barrier | Condom, which also protects against sexually transmitted diseases |
| Hormonal | Oral pills that change the hormone balance so eggs are not released |
| Intrauterine device | Copper-T or loop placed in the uterus |
| Surgical | Vasectomy (vas deferens blocked) and tubectomy (fallopian tube blocked) |

- **Sexually transmitted diseases:** bacterial, such as gonorrhoea and syphilis; viral, such as warts and **HIV-AIDS**.
- **Prenatal sex determination** is **prohibited by law** in India, because it has been misused to kill unborn girl children (**female foeticide**), which has led to a falling sex ratio.

## Five-minute revision checklist

- DNA copying is not exact → variation → survival of the species.
- Amoeba binary, Plasmodium multiple, Spirogyra fragmentation.
- Hydra: budding and regeneration; Planaria: regeneration; yeast: budding.
- Rhizopus spores in sporangia; Bryophyllum leaf buds; banana, orange, rose, jasmine: no seeds.
- Pollination = pollen to stigma; fertilisation = germ cell + egg. Ovule → seed, ovary → fruit.
- Testes in the scrotum for lower temperature; fertilisation in the oviduct; placenta feeds the embryo.
- Four contraception types; prenatal sex determination is banned.
