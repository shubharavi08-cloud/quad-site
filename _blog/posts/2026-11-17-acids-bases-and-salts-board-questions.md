## The three rules you must know first

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Litmus | Acids turn **blue litmus red**; bases turn **red litmus blue** |
| pH | Below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic. **Lower pH = more H⁺ ions** |
| Nature of a salt | Strong acid + strong base → **neutral**; weak acid + strong base → **basic**; strong acid + weak base → **acidic** |

## Type 1: Reverse the litmus change (1 mark)

> An aqueous solution turns red litmus blue. Excess addition of which of the following would reverse the change? (a) Sodium hydroxide (b) Hydrochloric acid (c) Baking soda (d) Slaked lime

- Red litmus turning blue means the solution is **basic**.
- To reverse it we need an **acid**. NaOH, baking soda and slaked lime are all basic.
- Excess HCl first neutralises the base, then makes the solution acidic.
- **Answer: (b) Hydrochloric acid.**

**Trap:** picking baking soda. A solution of NaHCO₃ is basic, so it cannot reverse a base.

## Type 2: What is baking powder? (1 mark)

> Baking powder is a mixture of: (a) sodium carbonate + acetic acid (b) sodium carbonate + tartaric acid (c) sodium hydrogen carbonate + tartaric acid (d) sodium hydrogen carbonate + acetic acid

- Baking soda is **sodium hydrogen carbonate, NaHCO₃**.
- Baking powder = baking soda + a mild, dry, edible acid: **tartaric acid**.
- When wet or heated it gives **CO₂**, which makes cakes soft and spongy.
- **Answer: (c).**

**Trap:** acetic acid is a liquid (vinegar); baking powder is a dry powder. **Try it:** why does a cake made with only baking soda taste bitter? *Sodium carbonate forms on heating, and it is bitter.*

## Type 3: Which salt is matched correctly? (1 mark)

> Salt · acid used · base used · nature: (a) NaCl · HCl · NaOH · basic (b) Na₂CO₃ · H₂CO₃ · NaOH · neutral (c) Na₂SO₄ · H₂SO₄ · NaOH · acidic (d) CH₃COONa · CH₃COOH · NaOH · basic

- HCl + NaOH are both strong, so NaCl is **neutral**, not basic. (a) is wrong.
- H₂CO₃ is a weak acid, so Na₂CO₃ is **basic**. (b) is wrong.
- H₂SO₄ + NaOH gives a **neutral** salt. (c) is wrong.
- CH₃COOH is weak and NaOH is strong, so sodium acetate is **basic**.
- **Answer: (d).**

**Trap:** choosing (a). Common salt is neutral. **Try it:** NH₄Cl is made from HCl and NH₄OH. Acidic, basic or neutral? *Acidic (strong acid + weak base).*

## Type 4: Copper oxide in dilute HCl (1 mark)

> A little copper oxide is stirred with dilute hydrochloric acid. The colour of the solution changes because of the formation of: (a) copper(II) oxide, black (b) copper(I) chloride, blue (c) copper(II) chloride, blue-green (d) copper(I) oxide, black

- A metal oxide is **basic**: metal oxide + acid → salt + water.
- **CuO + 2HCl → CuCl₂ + H₂O**. Copper(II) chloride gives a **blue-green** solution.
- **Answer: (c).**

**Trap:** option (a) is what you started with. The colour comes from the new product.

## Type 5: Case study on the pH scale (4 marks)

> (a) Which indicator tells how strong an acid or base is? (b) Solutions A, B and C have pH 3, 5 and 7. Which has the highest H⁺ concentration? (c) What are strong and weak acids?

- (a) **Universal indicator**, a mixture of indicators that shows a different colour across pH 0 to 14. Litmus only says acid or base.
- (b) **A (pH 3)**. The smaller the pH, the more H⁺ ions.
- (c) A **strong acid** gives more H⁺ ions in water (e.g. HCl); a **weak acid** gives fewer (e.g. CH₃COOH, acetic acid).

**Trap:** choosing C because 7 is the biggest number. **Try it:** fresh milk has pH about 6. As it sets into curd, does its pH rise or fall? *It falls; curd is more acidic.*

## Type 6: Plaster of Paris (3 marks)

> (a) Chemical name and formula of Plaster of Paris. (b) Equation for its preparation. (c) Two uses.

- (a) **Calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO₄·½H₂O.**
- (b) Heat gypsum at **373 K**: **CaSO₄·2H₂O → CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O**
- (c) Plaster for supporting fractured bones; making toys, decorative items and smooth surfaces.

**Trap:** writing CaSO₄·H₂O or leaving out 373 K. It is *half* a water molecule per formula unit. Store it in a moisture-proof container, because water makes it set back into hard gypsum.

## Type 7: Give reasons (3 marks)

> (i) To dilute an acid, acid is added to water and not water to acid. (ii) Dry HCl gas does not turn blue litmus red, but dilute hydrochloric acid does. (iii) Blue copper sulphate crystals turn white on heating.

- (i) Mixing an acid with water is **highly exothermic**. Water poured into acid can boil, splash and crack the glass. Add acid to water, slowly, with stirring.
- (ii) Acids show acidic behaviour only when they form **H⁺ (H₃O⁺) ions in water**. Dry HCl gives no H⁺ ions.
- (iii) Blue crystals are **CuSO₄·5H₂O**. Heating removes the **water of crystallisation**, leaving white anhydrous CuSO₄. Add a few drops of water and it turns blue again.

**Trap:** writing "dry HCl is a weak acid". HCl is a strong acid; it just has no H⁺ ions until water is present.

## One-minute revision

- Acids: blue litmus → red. Bases: red litmus → blue.
- pH below 7 acidic, 7 neutral, above 7 basic; lower pH = more H⁺.
- Metal oxides are basic; metal oxide + acid → salt + water.
- Baking soda NaHCO₃; baking powder = NaHCO₃ + tartaric acid; washing soda Na₂CO₃·10H₂O; bleaching powder CaOCl₂.
- Plaster of Paris CaSO₄·½H₂O from gypsum CaSO₄·2H₂O at 373 K.
- Always acid into water, never water into acid.

## FAQ

**Is Acids, Bases and Salts important for the CBSE board exam?**
Yes. Expect at least one MCQ on litmus, pH or the nature of a salt, and a 3-mark question on a named salt or a give-reasons set. Learn the formulas of the five salts above and you have most of the chapter.

**What is the quickest way to tell if a salt is acidic, basic or neutral?**
Find the acid and the base it was made from. If both are strong, the salt is neutral; if only the base is strong, it is basic; if only the acid is strong, it is acidic.
