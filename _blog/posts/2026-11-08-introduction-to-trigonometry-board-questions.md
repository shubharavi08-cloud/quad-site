Chapter 8 of CBSE Class 10 Maths, *Introduction to Trigonometry*, is one of the most reliable scorers in the paper. The same five kinds of question come back every year: find all the ratios from one, evaluate an expression with standard angles, find two angles from two equations, simplify, and prove an identity. None of them needs long working. Marks are lost on **signs**, on **squaring the wrong way**, and on **skipped steps in proofs**.

A quick way to rebuild the sin row in the exam hall: √0/2, √1/2, √2/2, √3/2, √4/2. The cos row is the same list read backwards.

## Type 1: One ratio given, find the others

> If sin A = 3/5, find cos A and tan A (A is acute).

Draw a right triangle with the side opposite A = 3 and the hypotenuse = 5.

- Adjacent side = √(5² − 3²) = √16 = 4
- cos A = **4/5**, tan A = 3/4

**Marking tip:** always show the Pythagoras line. It carries a mark on its own, and it is the step examiners look for first.

## Type 2: Evaluate with standard angles

> Evaluate: 2 tan² 45° + cos² 30° − sin² 60°

- tan 45° = 1, so 2 tan² 45° = 2
- cos² 30° = (√3/2)² = 3/4 and sin² 60° = (√3/2)² = 3/4
- Value = 2 + 3/4 − 3/4 = **2**

**Common mistake:** writing cos² 30° as cos 900°. The square belongs to the value, not to the angle: cos² 30° means (cos 30°)².

## Type 3: Two equations, two angles

> If sin (A + B) = 1 and cos (A − B) = √3/2, where 0° < A + B ≤ 90° and A > B, find A and B.

- sin (A + B) = 1 = sin 90°, so A + B = 90°
- cos (A − B) = √3/2 = cos 30°, so A − B = 30°
- Adding: 2A = 120°, so **A = 60°**, and **B = 30°**

**Check:** sin 90° = 1 and cos 30° = √3/2. Putting your answers back takes ten seconds and catches most slips.

## Type 4: Simplify using an identity

> Find the value of 9 sec² A − 9 tan² A.

- Take 9 common: 9 (sec² A − tan² A)
- Since 1 + tan² A = sec² A, the bracket is 1
- Value = **9**

**Remember the three identities:** sin² A + cos² A = 1, 1 + tan² A = sec² A, and 1 + cot² A = cosec² A. Almost every simplification MCQ uses one of them.

## Type 5: Prove an identity

> Prove that (sec A − tan A)² = (1 − sin A) / (1 + sin A).

Start from the left-hand side and write everything in sin and cos.

- LHS = (1/cos A − sin A/cos A)² = (1 − sin A)² / cos² A
- Replace cos² A by 1 − sin² A = (1 − sin A)(1 + sin A)
- LHS = (1 − sin A)² / [(1 − sin A)(1 + sin A)] = (1 − sin A) / (1 + sin A) = RHS

**Marking tip:** work on one side only, write each step on a new line, and end with "= RHS". A proof that moves terms across the equals sign can lose marks even when the algebra is right.

## A four-step routine for every question

1. Convert everything to sin and cos if you are stuck.
2. Look for a² − b² patterns: they hide (1 − sin A)(1 + sin A) and sec² − tan² = 1.
3. Keep surds exact until the last line.
4. Substitute your answer back, or check one side against the other, before moving on.

## FAQ

**Are complementary-angle questions (like sin 18° / cos 72°) still asked?**
That topic was removed from the rationalised NCERT book, so current Chapter 8 questions focus on ratios, standard angles and identities. Always check the latest CBSE syllabus and sample paper for your exam year.

**Do I have to memorise the whole table?**
Learn the sin row with the √0/2 to √4/2 trick, read cos backwards, and get tan by dividing sin by cos. That is enough to rebuild every value in under a minute.

**Can I start a proof from the right-hand side?**
Yes. Start from whichever side is more complicated and simplify it until it matches the other side.
