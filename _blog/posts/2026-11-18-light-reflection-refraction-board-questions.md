## The four rules you must know first

| Rule | What it means |
|---|---|
| Sign convention | Distances are measured from the pole / optical centre. **Object distance u is always negative** (the object is in front, against the light). |
| Focal length sign | Concave mirror and concave lens: **f negative**. Convex mirror and convex lens: **f positive**. |
| Formulas | Mirror: **1/v + 1/u = 1/f**, m = −v/u. Lens: **1/v − 1/u = 1/f**, m = v/u. |
| Sign of m | **Negative m → real and inverted. Positive m → virtual and erect.** If the size of m is more than 1, the image is magnified. |

## Type 1: Where to put the object for a virtual image (1 mark)

> Rays from the sun converge at a point 25 cm behind a convex lens. The distance at which an object should be placed in front of the lens to get a virtual image is: (a) 20 cm (b) 40 cm (c) 50 cm (d) more than 50 cm

- The sun's rays are parallel, so they meet at the **focus**: f = 25 cm.
- A convex lens gives a virtual image only when the object is **between F and the optical centre**.
- So the object must be closer than 25 cm. Only 20 cm fits.
- **Answer: (a) 20 cm.**

**Trap:** picking 50 cm. That is 2F, which gives a real image of the same size.

## Type 2: Negative magnification greater than one (1 mark)

> Which option gives negative magnification greater than one? (a) concave mirror, real image (b) concave mirror, virtual image (c) convex mirror, real image (d) convex mirror, virtual image

- Negative m means **inverted**, so the image is **real**.
- Magnification greater than one (in size) means **enlarged**.
- A convex mirror never forms a real image, so (c) is out.
- A concave mirror with the object between F and C gives a real, inverted, enlarged image.
- **Answer: (a).**

**Trap:** picking (b). A virtual image always has a **positive** magnification.

## Type 3: Refractive index and the speed of light (2 marks)

> (a) Define absolute refractive index. (b) Media A, B, C, D have refractive indices 1.50, 1.46, 1.31 and 1.77. Arrange them in increasing order of the speed of light in them.

- (a) Absolute refractive index **n = c / v**, the speed of light in vacuum divided by its speed in the medium.
- (b) Since v = c / n, a **bigger n means slower light**.
- Slowest D (1.77), then A (1.50), then B (1.46), fastest C (1.31).
- **Answer: D < A < B < C.**

**Trap:** sorting n from small to large. That gives the order of *decreasing* speed.

## Type 4: Convex lens, object at 2F (3 marks)

> An object is placed 30 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 15 cm. Use the lens formula to find the position of the image. What is the size of the image?

- u = −30 cm, f = +15 cm.
- 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/15 − 1/30 = 1/30, so **v = +30 cm**.
- m = v/u = 30 / (−30) = **−1**.
- **Answer:** image 30 cm behind the lens (at 2F), **real, inverted and the same size** as the object.

**Trap:** writing u = +30. The object is in front of the lens, so u is **negative**. **Try it:** if the object moves to 20 cm, where is the image? *v = +60 cm, beyond 2F and enlarged.*

## Type 5: Concave lens, image distance and height (3 marks)

> A 6 cm tall object is placed 30 cm from a concave lens of focal length 15 cm. Find (a) the image distance and (b) the image height.

- u = −30 cm, **f = −15 cm** (concave), h = 6 cm.
- 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = −1/15 − 1/30 = −3/30 = −1/10, so **v = −10 cm**.
- m = v/u = (−10)/(−30) = +1/3, so h′ = 6 × 1/3 = **2 cm**.
- **Answer:** image 10 cm from the lens on the object's side, 2 cm tall: **virtual, erect, diminished**.

**Trap:** taking f = +15 for a concave lens. A concave (diverging) lens has a **negative** focal length. **Try it:** what is its power? *P = 1/f = 1/(−0.15 m), about −6.7 D.*

## Type 6: Convex lens as a magnifier (3 marks)

> (a) Write the magnification of a lens in terms of u and v. (b) A 4 cm tall object is placed 10 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. Find the size of the image.

- (a) **m = h′/h = v/u.**
- (b) u = −10 cm, f = +20 cm: 1/v = 1/20 − 1/10 = −1/20, so **v = −20 cm**.
- m = (−20)/(−10) = **+2**, so h′ = 2 × 4 = **8 cm**.
- **Answer:** 8 cm tall, **virtual, erect, magnified** (this is how a magnifying glass works).

**Trap:** using the mirror formula m = −v/u for a lens. You would get −2 and a wrong "inverted" image.

## Type 7: The rear-view mirror (3 marks)

> A rear-view mirror of a car has a focal length of 2.0 m. A car behind is 8.0 m from the mirror. (a) Find the position of its image. (b) Draw the ray diagram.

- A rear-view mirror is **convex**, so f = +2.0 m; u = −8.0 m.
- 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/2 + 1/8 = 5/8, so **v = +1.6 m**.
- Positive v means the image is **behind the mirror**: virtual and erect. m = −v/u = 1.6/8 = 0.2, so it is **diminished**.
- **Answer:** 1.6 m behind the mirror, virtual, erect, one-fifth the size.
- Diagram: a ray parallel to the axis reflects as if from F behind the mirror; a ray aimed at C reflects back on itself. Extend both (dotted) behind the mirror; they meet at the image.

**Trap:** taking f = −2 m. Convex mirrors have a **positive** focal length. **Try it:** why are convex mirrors used as rear-view mirrors? *They always give an erect image and a much wider field of view.*

## One-minute revision

- u is always negative. Convex mirror and convex lens: f positive. Concave: f negative.
- Mirror: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f, m = −v/u. Lens: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f, m = v/u.
- Negative m = real and inverted. Positive m = virtual and erect.
- Convex lens: object inside F gives a virtual, magnified image; object at 2F gives a same-size real image.
- Concave lens and convex mirror: always virtual, erect, diminished.
- n = c/v: higher n, slower light. P = 1/f (f in metres), in dioptres.

## FAQ

**What is the easiest way to avoid sign mistakes?**
Before you substitute, write one line: "u = −…, f = ±…" and decide the sign of f from the type of mirror or lens. Most wrong answers in this chapter come from skipping that line.
