## The four ideas you must know first

| Idea | In one line |
|---|---|
| **Biodegradable** | Broken down by bacteria and fungi (paper, wood, peels, cotton) |
| **Non-biodegradable** | Not broken down by microbes (DDT, plastics, glass, metal cans) |
| **10 percent law** | Only about **10%** of the energy at one trophic level reaches the next |
| **Biological magnification** | Non-biodegradable chemicals **accumulate** at each level, so the top level has the most |

A **trophic level** is a step in a food chain: producers (green plants) are the first level, herbivores the second, carnivores the third, and so on.

## Type 1: Which pollutant never breaks down? (1 mark)

> Which of the following is a non-biodegradable pollutant? (a) Paper (b) DDT (c) Wood (d) Vegetable peel

- Biodegradable substances are broken down by bacteria and fungi.
- Paper and wood are plant material, and vegetable peel is plant matter too: all biodegradable.
- DDT is a man-made chemical pesticide that microbes cannot break down.
- **Answer: (b) DDT.**

**Trap:** picking paper because it feels "man-made". Paper is made from wood pulp, so microbes break it down. **Try it:** is a cotton cloth biodegradable? *Yes.*

## Type 2: Why food chains stay short (1 mark)

> Which of the following limits the number of trophic levels in a food chain? (a) Availability of water (b) Deficient food supply (c) Decrease in energy at higher levels (d) Pollution in air

- Suppose producers trap 1000 J of the Sun's energy.
- Only about 10% passes on at each step: 1000 → 100 → 10 → 1 J.
- After three or four levels there is too little energy left to support another level.
- **Answer: (c) Decrease in energy at higher levels.**

**Trap:** picking "deficient food supply". Food is there; it is the **energy** available at each higher level that keeps shrinking. **Try it:** if producers have 20,000 J, how much reaches the third trophic level? *200 J.*

## Type 3: Which change does not harm the environment? (1 mark)

> (i) Changes in our life-styles have resulted in greater amounts of waste material generation. (ii) Changes in attitude have resulted in use of more disposable things. (iii) Changes in packaging have resulted in much of our waste becoming non-biodegradable. (iv) Organic farming. Which of these is not leading to any damage of the environment? (a) (i), (ii), (iii) (b) (iv) (c) (i) and (iii) (d) (ii) and (iv)

- (i) more waste, (ii) more disposable things and (iii) non-biodegradable packaging all damage the environment.
- (iv) Organic farming avoids chemical fertilisers and pesticides, so it does not pollute soil and water.
- **Answer: (b) (iv) only.**

**Trap:** picking (d) because it contains (iv). Option (d) also contains (ii), which **does** harm the environment. In "which of these" questions, every item in the option must be right. **Try it:** give one way to cut non-biodegradable waste at home. *Carry a cloth bag instead of taking plastic bags.*

## Type 4: Assertion and Reason: pesticides at the top (1 mark)

> **Assertion (A):** The highest percentage of pesticides is found in tertiary consumers in a food chain. **Reason (R):** Chemicals or pesticides get accumulated progressively at each trophic level.

- Pesticides wash into soil and water and are absorbed by plants.
- They are non-biodegradable, so they are not broken down or passed out of the body.
- Each level eats many organisms from the level below, so the chemical piles up at every step. This is **biological magnification**: R is true.
- The top level (the tertiary consumers) ends up with the most: A is true.
- **Answer: (a) Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.**

**Trap:** choosing (b). R is exactly **why** the top level has the most, so it is the correct explanation. **Try it:** in plants → fish → bird → humans, who carries the highest DDT concentration? *Humans, at the highest trophic level.*

## Type 5: 5 J for man: how much for the plant? (2 marks)

> In the food chain Plant → Goat → Man, 5 J of energy is available to man. Calculate how much energy was available at the producer level. Why?

- By the 10% law, man's 5 J is 10% of what the goat had, so the goat had **50 J**.
- The goat's 50 J is 10% of what the plant had, so the plant had **500 J**.
- Why: at each level about 90% of the energy is used for the organism's own life processes or lost as heat.
- **Answer: 500 J at the producer level.**

**Trap:** dividing instead of multiplying and writing 0.05 J for the plant. Producers always have the **most** energy. Going up the chain, divide by 10; going back down to the producers, multiply by 10. **Try it:** a snake gets 2 J in grass → grasshopper → frog → snake. How much did the grass have? *2000 J.*

## Type 6: Decomposers, nature's cleaners (2 marks)

> What are decomposers? List two important roles they play in the environment.

- **Decomposers** are microorganisms such as **bacteria and fungi** that break down the complex organic substances of dead plants, animals and wastes into **simple inorganic substances**.
- **Role 1:** they clean the environment of dead remains and wastes.
- **Role 2:** they return nutrients to the soil, so plants can use them again.

**Trap:** writing that decomposers turn inorganic substances into organic ones. That is what **producers** (green plants) do. **Try it:** what would happen if all decomposers disappeared? *Dead matter would pile up and nutrients would not return to the soil.*

## Type 7: CFCs, the ozone layer and the 1987 agreement (2 marks)

> (i) Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs) have been linked to depletion of the ozone layer. In 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) succeeded in making an agreement. State this agreement. (ii) What is the role of ozone in our atmosphere?

- How ozone forms: high-energy ultraviolet (UV) radiation splits oxygen, O₂ → O + O, and the free oxygen atoms join oxygen molecules, O + O₂ → O₃ (ozone).
- CFCs, used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers, destroy this ozone.
- (i) In 1987 UNEP forged an agreement to **freeze CFC production at 1986 levels**.
- (ii) The ozone layer in the upper atmosphere **shields the Earth's surface from harmful UV radiation** of the Sun, which can cause skin cancer in human beings.

**Trap:** writing that ozone blocks infrared radiation or "heat". It blocks **ultraviolet** radiation. **Try it:** is ozone at ground level helpful? *No, at ground level it is a deadly poison.*

## One-minute revision

- Biodegradable: broken down by microbes. Non-biodegradable: DDT, plastics, glass, metal cans.
- Trophic levels: producers → herbivores → carnivores; food chains are usually only 3 or 4 levels long.
- 10% law: divide by 10 going up the chain, multiply by 10 coming back to the producers.
- Biological magnification: non-biodegradable chemicals are highest at the top level (often humans).
- Decomposers (bacteria, fungi): complex organic → simple inorganic; they clean up and recycle nutrients.
- Ozone: O₃, made when UV acts on O₂; shields us from UV; CFCs deplete it; UNEP 1987: freeze CFC production at 1986 levels.

## FAQ

**Is "Our Environment" still in the CBSE Class 10 syllabus?**
Yes. In the current NCERT book it is Chapter 13, the last chapter of Science, and it is usually tested with 1 and 2 mark questions like the seven above.

**How do I remember which way to multiply in energy questions?**
Draw the chain left to right with the producer first. Each arrow to the right means "divide by 10". If the question gives you the top and asks for the producer, walk back to the left and multiply by 10 at every arrow.
