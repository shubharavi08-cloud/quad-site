Chapter 5 of CBSE Class 10 Science, *Life Processes*, is the longest biology chapter in the book, and it rewards students who can **draw, label and explain in order**. Most lost marks come from three habits: **mixing up where each step happens** (cytoplasm or mitochondria, stomach or small intestine), **skipping the reason** in "why" questions, and **unlabelled diagrams**.

## Type 1: Photosynthesis, its equation and its three events

> Write the balanced equation for photosynthesis and list the events that take place during the process.

6CO₂ + 12H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ + 6H₂O (in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll)

The three events:

- **Absorption of light energy** by chlorophyll.
- **Conversion of light energy into chemical energy** and **splitting of water** into hydrogen and oxygen.
- **Reduction of carbon dioxide** to carbohydrates.

**Experiment question:** a variegated leaf (green and white patches) from a destarched plant is kept in sunlight, then tested with iodine. Only the **green parts turn blue-black**, which shows that **chlorophyll is needed** for photosynthesis.

**Try it:** why are desert plants said to take up CO₂ at night? Their stomata stay closed in the day to save water, so they take in CO₂ at night and store it as an intermediate, using it in the day.

## Type 2: How stomata open and close

> What are the functions of guard cells?

- Each stoma is a tiny pore surrounded by two **guard cells**.
- When water flows **into** the guard cells, they swell and the pore **opens**.
- When they lose water, they shrink and the pore **closes**.
- Stomata allow **gas exchange** and also control **water loss** by transpiration.

**Common mistake:** writing "stomata make food". Food is made in the chloroplasts of the leaf cells; stomata only let gases in and out.

## Type 3: Which juice does what in digestion

This is a favourite table-style question. Learn it as a journey through the alimentary canal:

| Where | Juice / gland | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | Saliva (salivary amylase) | Breaks starch into simple sugar |
| Stomach | Gastric glands: HCl, pepsin, mucus | HCl makes the medium acidic; pepsin digests proteins; mucus protects the stomach lining |
| Small intestine | Bile from the liver | Makes the food alkaline and breaks fat into small globules (emulsification) |
| Small intestine | Pancreatic juice: trypsin, lipase | Trypsin digests proteins; lipase digests emulsified fats |
| Small intestine wall | Intestinal juice | Completes digestion: proteins to amino acids, carbohydrates to glucose, fats to fatty acids and glycerol |

**Try it:** what are villi and why are they useful? Finger-like projections of the small intestine wall; they **increase the surface area for absorption** and are richly supplied with blood vessels.

## Type 4: Aerobic and anaerobic respiration

> Show the different ways in which glucose is broken down in organisms.

- In every case, **glucose (6 carbon) is first broken into pyruvate (3 carbon) in the cytoplasm**.
- **No oxygen, in yeast:** pyruvate → ethanol + carbon dioxide + energy (fermentation).
- **Lack of oxygen, in our muscle cells:** pyruvate → lactic acid + energy. The build-up of lactic acid causes **muscle cramps**.
- **With oxygen, in mitochondria:** pyruvate → carbon dioxide + water + energy. This releases far more energy.

The energy is stored as **ATP**, the energy currency of the cell.

**Marking tip:** the mark is often for writing the **place** (cytoplasm or mitochondria) next to each step. Draw the three arrows from pyruvate and label them.

**Try it:** why do aquatic animals breathe much faster than land animals? The amount of oxygen dissolved in water is much lower than in air.

## Type 5: Why the human heart has four chambers

> What is double circulation, and why is it necessary in mammals and birds?

- Blood passes through the heart **twice** in each cycle: once to the lungs (pulmonary) and once to the body (systemic).
- The right and left sides of the heart are separate, so **oxygenated and deoxygenated blood do not mix**.
- This gives a highly efficient supply of oxygen, which mammals and birds need because they use a lot of energy to **keep their body temperature constant**.

**Compare:** fish have a **two-chambered** heart and blood passes through the heart only once in each cycle. Amphibians and many reptiles have **three chambers** and can tolerate some mixing.

## Type 6: Xylem versus phloem

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Carries | Water and minerals | Food (sucrose and other products of photosynthesis) |
| Direction | Roots upward to leaves | In both directions, to wherever food is needed |
| Driven by | Root pressure and transpiration pull | Energy from ATP; sucrose raises the osmotic pressure so water moves in |
| Name of process | Transport of water | Translocation |

**Try it:** why is transpiration useful to a plant? It helps pull water and minerals up from the roots, and it helps keep the plant cool.

## Type 7: The nephron and how urine forms (5 marks)

> Draw a labelled diagram of a nephron and describe how urine is produced.

- Blood enters the **glomerulus**, a cluster of capillaries inside **Bowman's capsule**, where it is **filtered**.
- As the filtrate moves along the tubule, useful substances such as **glucose, amino acids, salts and a large amount of water are selectively reabsorbed**.
- What remains is **urine**. It passes from the kidneys through the **ureters** to the **urinary bladder** and leaves through the **urethra**.
- The kidneys make about **180 litres** of initial filtrate a day, but only **a litre or two** is excreted as urine. The rest is reabsorbed.

**Labels to show:** glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, tubule, collecting duct, and the blood vessels around the tubule.

**Try it:** what is dialysis? When the kidneys fail, an **artificial kidney** removes nitrogenous waste from the blood. Unlike a real kidney, it involves no reabsorption.

## Five-minute revision checklist

- Photosynthesis: light absorbed, water split, CO₂ reduced. Iodine test: blue-black = starch.
- Guard cells swell: stoma opens. Guard cells shrink: stoma closes.
- Mouth: amylase. Stomach: HCl, pepsin, mucus. Small intestine: bile, trypsin, lipase. Villi absorb.
- Glucose → pyruvate (cytoplasm) → ethanol + CO₂ (yeast) / lactic acid (muscle) / CO₂ + H₂O (mitochondria).
- Four chambers, two circuits, no mixing.
- Xylem: water up. Phloem: food both ways, uses ATP.
- Nephron: filter in the glomerulus, reabsorb in the tubule.
