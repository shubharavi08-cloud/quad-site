Chapter 9 of CBSE Class 10 Maths, *Some Applications of Trigonometry*, usually brings one 3-mark or 4-mark heights-and-distances problem, and sometimes a case-study question built on the same idea. The trigonometry itself is light: you only need tan, sin and cos of 30°, 45° and 60°. Marks are lost on the **diagram**, on mixing up **elevation and depression**, and on **rounding too early**.

This guide works through the four question types that come up again and again, with model answers. Read each question, **pause and try it yourself**, then check.

Use √3 = 1.732 only in the last line, if the question asks for a decimal answer.

## Type 1: One right triangle (the kite string)

> A kite is flying at a height of 60 m above the ground. The string attached to the kite is temporarily tied to a point on the ground. The inclination of the string with the ground is 60°. Find the length of the string, assuming there is no slack.

**Diagram:** the string is the hypotenuse, the height 60 m is the side opposite the 60° angle.

- sin 60° = height / string, so √3/2 = 60 / L
- L = 120 / √3 = **40√3 m ≈ 69.28 m**

**Marking tip:** choose the ratio that links the side you know with the side you want. Here that is sin (opposite and hypotenuse), not tan.

## Type 2: Walking towards a tower (two angles, one height)

> The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a point on the ground is 30°. After walking 20 m towards the tower, the angle of elevation becomes 60°. Find the height of the tower.

Let the height be h and the distance from the nearer point to the foot of the tower be x.

- From the nearer point: tan 60° = h / x, so **h = √3 x**
- From the first point: tan 30° = h / (x + 20), so **h = (x + 20) / √3**
- Equate: √3 x = (x + 20) / √3, so 3x = x + 20, giving **x = 10 m**
- Height h = 10√3 m **≈ 17.32 m**

**Marking tip:** write both equations before solving. Examiners give a mark for each correct equation even if the final arithmetic slips.

## Type 3: Angle of depression (two ships from a lighthouse)

> As observed from the top of a 75 m high lighthouse, the angles of depression of two ships are 30° and 45°. If one ship is exactly behind the other on the same side of the lighthouse, find the distance between the two ships.

The angle of depression at the top equals the angle of elevation at the ship (alternate angles, because the horizontal line at the top is parallel to the ground). So you can work with the angles at the ships.

- Nearer ship (45°): tan 45° = 75 / d₁, so d₁ = 75 m
- Farther ship (30°): tan 30° = 75 / d₂, so d₂ = 75√3 m
- Distance between the ships = d₂ − d₁ = **75(√3 − 1) m ≈ 54.9 m**

**Common mistake:** putting the depression angle inside the triangle at the top, between the lighthouse and the line of sight. That angle is 90° minus the depression angle. Draw the horizontal line at the top first, then mark the angle below it.

## Type 4: Building and tower (elevation and depression together)

> From the top of a 7 m high building, the angle of elevation of the top of a cable tower is 60° and the angle of depression of its foot is 45°. Determine the height of the tower.

Draw a horizontal line from the top of the building to the tower. It splits the tower into two parts: 7 m below the line and h above it. Call the distance between the building and the tower d.

- Depression of the foot (45°): tan 45° = 7 / d, so **d = 7 m**
- Elevation of the top (60°): tan 60° = h / d, so h = 7√3 m
- Height of the tower = 7 + 7√3 = **7(√3 + 1) m ≈ 19.12 m**

**Marking tip:** the final answer is the whole tower, not just the part above the horizontal line. Many students stop at 7√3.

## A five-step routine for every problem

1. Draw the figure and label the known lengths and angles.
2. Draw the horizontal line wherever there is an angle of depression.
3. Mark the unknowns with letters (h, x, d).
4. Write one trigonometric equation per right triangle.
5. Solve, keep √3 in surd form, and convert to a decimal only at the end.

## FAQ

**Is the angle of depression always equal to an angle of elevation?**
When the observer and the object are at different heights and the ground is level, yes: the angle of depression from the top equals the angle of elevation from the bottom, because they are alternate angles between parallel horizontal lines.

**Should I leave the answer as 10√3 or write 17.32?**
If the question gives "use √3 = 1.732" or asks for the answer to two decimal places, give the decimal. Otherwise the surd form is exact and fully acceptable. Writing both is safe.

**How many marks does this chapter carry?**
